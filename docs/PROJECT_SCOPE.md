# EdgeLab v3.6 candidate: reliability operations and recorded coordination evidence

Goal: evolve the interactive resilience lab into a complete, self-hostable reliability application with an operating service, durable history, an operator workflow, and reproducible evidence.

Acceptance requirements (completion requires evidence for every item):

- A real scheduled Worker checks deployment-approved HTTPS services and private service bindings without an open browser. Public callers cannot choose targets or trigger probes.
- SQLite-backed monitoring persists checks, service policies, incidents, acknowledgement, and audit events across eviction and deployment. Existing laboratory data remains compatible.
- Duplicate/retried/overlapping schedules cannot double-count samples or create duplicate incidents. Leases recover interrupted work; stale completions cannot overwrite changed policies.
- Incidents open after consecutive bad observations, recover after consecutive good observations, and permit authenticated acknowledgement with a bounded operator note.
- Reporting includes observed good-check ratio, latency percentiles, error-budget consumption, missing-sample coverage, freshness, and explicit measurement limits. Gaps never become successful checks.
- Scheduled checks start only in the current UTC minute. Delayed invocations are skipped with persisted diagnostic evidence and never backfill history. Each new check retains its actual start timestamp; a completion crossing a minute boundary keeps the original slot. Migrated checks with unknown start time remain visible but receive no verified SLO credit.
- `GET /api/ready` evaluates persisted scheduler completion and active-service freshness against an inclusive three-minute bound. Only healthy readiness returns 200; starting, partial, and stalled return 503. Paused services are ignored, and dashboard reads cannot renew the evidence.
- Fixed rule version 1 evaluates sampled-check burn over paired 60/5-minute windows at 14.4×, 360/30 at 6×, and 4,320/360 at 1×, based on the Google SRE 30-day budget examples. Both windows must meet the threshold. Signals remain separate from consecutive-failure incident transitions and external notifications.
- Each signal window exposes its exact finished-minute range, full expected minutes, verified good/bad observations, maintenance, unknown slots, coverage, burn, and current-policy maturity. Missing, legacy, wrong-revision, incorrectly timed, and conflicting duplicate observations cannot improve evidence; exact duplicates count once.
- Explicit project gates require full current-policy windows, at least 95% verified coverage in each, at least 20 non-maintenance observations in the long window, and five in the short window. Current pause or fully verified all-maintenance pairs show maintenance; mixed maintenance cannot bypass sample gates.
- A qualified firing rule takes priority over insufficient longer-window history; highest priority is rapid, then sustained, then gradual. A partially qualified rule set never conceals firing or becomes a confident all-clear result. The one-minute sampling granularity and the 99.9% target's coarse single-failure signal are documented without claiming customer-request availability.
- The coordinator computes and persists signals after scheduled probes complete. Public reads and exports expose the latest evaluation without reevaluating it. Same-revision evidence is current through an inclusive 180,000-millisecond bound; stale, changed-policy, and not-evaluated states cannot imply clearance.
- One last firing record per configured service survives insufficient evidence, maintenance, policy changes, stale scheduling, and later clear results. The UI distinguishes historical warning evidence from current firing and same-policy qualified clearance. Signals do not resolve incidents or send notifications.
- Warning evidence captures a self-contained, public-safe policy version with original service name, good-check target, latency/timeout, contract, transport, recorded time, and provenance. It survives pruning of 30-day checks and unreferenced source versions. Older warnings explicitly report unavailable context; reads never fill the gap from mutable current settings. New confirmation can capture a matching version without asserting that a legacy warning had context at its initial firing.
- The browser continues aging displayed budget evidence when status refreshes fail, without asserting that the server's scheduler stopped. A confirmed operator write remains confirmed if its follow-up status/audit read fails. Uncertain note submissions keep their request ID and exact body in authenticated Operations memory across investigation close/reopen; Lock, credential changes, leaving Operations, and reload clear that private session state.
- Public incident lists include every open incident for active deployment targets plus their latest 100 resolved incidents. `GET /api/ops/incidents/<id>?before=<slot>` provides 50 descending retained checks per page, lifecycle timestamps, retention bounds, and policy versions referenced on the page. Older open incidents cannot be hidden by a recent resolved-record limit.
- Policy versions captured at enrollment and subsequent changes preserve their context with `recorded` provenance. Migration labels the available current policy `recovered-current` and does not invent older policy history. Missing historical versions remain unknown.
- A valid bearer token adds private evidence to incident detail. `POST /api/ops/incident-note` supports a UUID v4 request ID, a 1–500-character nonblank note, at most 100 retained notes per incident, and idempotent retries of an identical payload. Conflicting reuse returns 409; recovered incidents permit follow-up notes.
- Public operations UI includes service health, history, incidents, reliability trends, export, and methodology; owner controls support policy editing, maintenance pause, and acknowledgement. The laboratory remains available.
- Operator writes require a deployment secret, same-origin browser requests, bounded and validated payloads. Public data excludes secrets, private target URLs, and operator notes. Probe targets are deploy-time configuration, redirects are not followed, time and response consumption are bounded.
- Authenticated incident briefs freeze the newest public-safe page of at most 50 observations, its referenced policy versions, lifecycle, deterministic facts and sampling limits with a canonical SHA-256 hash. The selected page can omit older incident history. Full frozen citations remain inspectable after source rows change or are pruned, with legacy timing, missing context, retention loss and unfinished minutes explicit.
- Brief coverage measures only finished minutes between the oldest and newest selected observations and excludes verified maintenance. Empty/current-only or maintenance-only slices have null coverage. These facts never imply complete-incident coverage or customer uptime. Model input uses representative entries within 2 KiB of message contents and 4 KiB of serialized input; selected citation IDs and the omitted-reference count distinguish input omissions from capture limits, older pages and retention loss.
- The server selects the active Llama 3.3 70B model, a versioned schema and a 512-token output bound. AI hypotheses are labeled unverified and cite only relevant IDs included in the bounded prompt. Strict local validation rejects malformed schema, arbitrary actions, unknown/omitted/unrelated citations, HTML, URLs and tool calls; a relevant citation cannot prove cause. Suggestions are never executed and cannot acknowledge, recover or modify incidents.
- Durable UUID request ownership precedes inference. Identical retries retrieve pending or terminal records without redispatch; conflicting incident reuse returns 409. Four reserved model attempts per UTC day, one start per UTC minute, one persisted pending attempt and a 20-second deadline bound this application. Expired ownership prevents late output publication; provider cancellation is best effort. No verified bad observation produces insufficient evidence without inference or attempt consumption. Missing capability is explicit and default inference is disabled until account-plan preflight. Shared account quota is not guaranteed. CI uses fake AI; a real model claim requires separate successful evidence.
- Independent brief-record admission permits at most 16 new creations per UTC day, 256 physically retained rows and 128 KiB per new serialized record with completion headroom. Deterministic records consume admission without inference; rows and daily reservations commit atomically, cleanup never refunds counters, retained UUID replay precedes limits, and counterless upgrades conservatively close the first UTC day without inventing deleted creation history.
- A public read-only explorer presents three pinned controlled evidence scenarios, human-authored canned explanations, relevant citations, capture/input provenance and insufficient-evidence skipping. Browsers can verify the original snapshot hash and compare an altered copy locally. These interactions make zero application API or native AI calls, reveal no operator data, and cannot change live monitoring. Canned acceptance is not a model-quality, authenticity, recovery or root-cause claim.
- Private brief history is retained for 30 days after creation and excluded from public snapshots/exports. Frozen citations remain inspectable after live checks or source policy versions change. Client request IDs survive investigation close/reopen in authenticated memory; Lock, credential changes, leaving Operations and reload clear private session state. Opening or polling a brief invokes only GET reads.
- Unit and actual-runtime tests cover incident state transitions, retries, concurrency, crash/lease recovery, policy races, auth, retention, and persistence. Existing lab checks continue passing.
- Metadata cleanup uses persisted FIFO queues with at most 32 version and 32 orphan-note candidates per completed cleanup. All retained check references, current policies and restored note parents protect their source rows. Mutation/replacement triggers and atomic one-time legacy backfill prevent missed candidates; deletion and dequeue roll back together. Observation/note age expiry and atomic removal of all notes from expired resolved parents remain eager, while other unused metadata is eventually collected. Reads do not drain queues, and warm-workload projections must include trigger/index/queue costs and metadata growth without claiming account-wide capacity.
- Version 3.4.2 attaches a bounded cleanup receipt to the existing completed scheduler event in the same source/queue transaction. Direct source deletions are counted through consumed `DELETE RETURNING 1`; examined equals deleted plus protected plus missing, and a full 32-candidate batch conservatively sets `mayRemain`. Eager expiry and cold migration are excluded. Public status and exports expose only version counts; authenticated audit adds private orphan-note counts through one indexed latest-completed lookup. Legacy/malformed/null receipts remain unavailable, cached timestamps never renew, and disclosures add no browser polling. Controlled whole-cron costs remain steady; full combined catch-up adds 128 reads with no additional writes or statements, not an account-capacity claim. See [ADR 007](adr/007-committed-cleanup-diagnostics.md) and [3.4.2 evidence](evidence/releases/3.4.2-retention-cost.json).
- Version 3.5 adds a same-origin, capability-scoped live lab observer on the existing Durable Object Hibernation WebSocket API. At most four read-only sockets attach to existing state; they never seed a run, renew a deadline, schedule an alarm or replay a command. Bounded allowlisted frames expose actual commits and at most twelve outcomes. Increasing revisions, reset/run fencing, real hibernation with original sockets, deadline races, transaction rollback, UI lifetimes and zero/one/four-viewer storage measurements require actual-runtime and browser evidence. Expired-owner revival starts a new run even before delayed alarm delivery. Successful production observation remains a separate gate from a quota-unavailable upgrade. See [ADR 008](adr/008-live-lab-observer.md).
- Candidate 3.6 records one accepted observer interval in browser memory, capped at 256 entries and 192 KiB with 1 KiB finalization headroom. Stop freezes the immutable valid prefix while live observation continues; Download exports it. Reconnect/leave/reload cannot preserve an undownloaded recording. Strict import validates shape, privacy, protocol, timestamp/counter bounds, revision/terminal order and an unsigned canonical content hash. Separate server and recorder clocks, legacy unknown times, revision gaps and capture endings remain explicit.
- The offline `#replay` route reads no capability, makes zero application API requests or WebSocket messages, adds no browser persistence and executes no recorded commands. Native keyboard controls inspect the selected prefix without interpolation or simulated refill. A bundled 25-frame actual local Worker trace demonstrates pending/settled work, reset fencing and original-socket hibernation; it is neither a production history nor a whole-run backup, authenticity proof or deterministic re-execution. [ADR 009](adr/009-bounded-lab-recordings.md) and the [runtime manifest](evidence/releases/3.6.0-recording-runtime.json) record the boundary.
- `npm run test:lab-recording` runs in CI against the real private origin and network peer with AI disabled, no account/production calls and all runtimes disposed. Local candidate checks pass 136 unit tests across 13 files and keyboard/mobile replay with zero application API requests. Browser file upload was denied by automation permissions, without bypass or retry; strict import rejection is covered by unit tests and is not claimed as completed browser upload verification.
- A repeatable benchmark reports measured concurrency behavior with environment and methodology, without invented production or global-scale claims.
- OpenAPI, operator runbook, architecture decisions, threat boundaries, CI, and deployment instructions match the shipped implementation.
- Operations exports use `schemaVersion: 4`, separate unverified checks from verified SLO metrics, and include readiness plus 20 recent persistent scheduler diagnostics. Version 3.2 adds `service.budget` without replacing the export contract. Diagnostics and appended notes have 30-day retention; open incident records and configured-service warning evidence remain retained, with evidence limits explicit.
- Publish verified code and the working upgrade; verify live scheduled samples and record the exact tested revision. Do not claim a long operating history immediately after deployment.

Implementation phases: monitoring domain and storage; scheduled/API integration; operations UI; failure tests and benchmark; incident evidence and policy history; timing correction and readiness; paired-window evaluation and durable warning evidence; self-contained warning context and operator session correctness; frozen incident briefs and bounded AI integration; live observation; bounded recording and offline inspection; documentation; deployment and completion audit. Published 3.5.0 remains the verified release. Non-goals: multi-tenant SaaS billing, external email/PagerDuty delivery, global multi-region uptime measurement, or a general-purpose unrestricted URL proxy. Incident notifications are visible in the application; no messages are sent to third parties.
